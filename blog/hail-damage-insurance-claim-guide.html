Storm & insurance

Hail just hit your neighborhood. Here is exactly what to do next.

AA Premier Roofing & Construction · 07-03-2026 · 6 min read · All guides

North Texas takes some of the heaviest hail in the country, and the days after a storm are when the most expensive mistakes happen. Here is the honest, step-by-step version of what to do, from a crew that walks these claims with homeowners every season.

Day 1: document before anything else

You do not need to get on the roof. From the ground and with your phone:

  • Photograph the date on your phone's weather app or a local news report of the storm.
  • Photograph hail on the ground next to something for scale (a coin or a tape measure).
  • Photograph dents on soft metal: gutters, downspouts, mailboxes, AC fins, car hoods.
  • Walk the yard and photograph any shingles, shingle granules, or flashing that came down.

Hail damage on the roof itself is often invisible from the ground. Bruised shingles do not always look like anything from your driveway, which is exactly why people miss it until it leaks months later.

Day 1 to 7: get a professional inspection before you file

Call a local roofer for an inspection before you call your insurance company. There are two reasons. First, if there is no real damage, you avoid filing a claim you did not need. Second, if there is damage, you file with photo documentation already in hand instead of guessing.

A legitimate roofer will document everything with photos, tell you plainly if the damage does not justify a claim, and give you that answer for free. Be careful with out-of-town crews that appear right after storms, knock on doors, and pressure you to sign something on the spot. Never sign anything that assigns your insurance benefits to a contractor you just met.

When you file: what to say

Call the claims number on your policy, give the storm date, and describe the documented damage. Ask two questions and write the answers down:

  • What is my deductible for wind and hail? In Texas this is often a percentage of your home's insured value, not a flat number.
  • What is my deadline to file and to complete repairs? Most policies have a filing window, commonly one year from the storm date, sometimes less.

The adjuster meeting: have your roofer there

Your insurer sends an adjuster to inspect the roof. You are allowed to have your contractor present, and you should. The adjuster is on your roof for a limited time; your roofer knows where the damage is and makes sure nothing legitimate gets missed or dismissed. This one meeting is where most claims are won or lost.

Reading the outcome

If the claim is approved, the insurer issues an estimate and payment, usually in two parts: an initial check, then the recoverable depreciation after the work is completed. Your out-of-pocket cost should generally be your deductible. Under Texas law, a contractor cannot legally offer to waive or absorb your deductible; treat any offer to do so as a red flag.

If the claim is denied and you believe the damage is real, you can request a re-inspection with your documentation, or ask your roofer to walk the denial with you. Sometimes a denial is correct and the honest answer is a repair you pay for directly, which is often cheaper than people fear.

The mistakes that sink honest claims

  • Waiting. Damage documented eleven months after a storm is much harder to tie to that storm.
  • Signing an assignment of benefits or a contingency agreement under pressure at the door.
  • Making permanent repairs before the adjuster sees the roof. Temporary tarping to stop a leak is fine and expected; replacing shingles is not.
  • Accepting a scope that misses obvious items because nobody who knew roofs was at the inspection.

The short version

Document the storm, get a free professional inspection before filing, have your roofer meet the adjuster, never pay more than your deductible, and never sign anything at the door.
