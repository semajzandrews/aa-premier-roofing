7 signs your DFW roof actually needs replacing (and 3 that just need a repair)

AA Premier Roofing & Construction · 07-03-2026 · 5 min read · All guides

Texas sun, heat cycles, and hail age a roof faster than almost anywhere in the country. A typical architectural shingle roof here often retires years before the number printed on its warranty. Here is how to tell the difference between a roof that needs work and a roof that needs to go.

Signs that point to replacement

1. Granules filling your gutters. Granules are the shingle's sunscreen. When they wash off in quantity and you see smooth, shiny patches on the shingles, the asphalt underneath is cooking in the sun and the end is coming regardless of leaks.

2. Curling or cupping shingle edges. Heat-aged shingles lift at the corners and no longer seal flat. Once curling is widespread, wind gets under them and repairs stop holding.

3. Widespread hail bruising. One or two impacts are a repair. Bruising across multiple slopes compromises the mat of the shingle itself, and the roof will fail early even where you cannot see it. This is the classic DFW insurance replacement.

4. Shingles going brittle. If a shingle cracks or snaps when lifted gently, the material has aged out. Brittle shingles cannot be repaired around, because every repair breaks the shingles next to it.

5. Sagging roof lines or soft decking. A dip in the roof line means the problem is below the shingles, in the decking or structure. That is not a shingle repair, and waiting makes it structural.

6. Daylight or water stains in the attic. Check the attic on a bright day. Pinholes of daylight, damp insulation, or brown rings on the decking mean water has been getting through for a while.

7. Your roof is 15 to 20+ years old and any of the above is starting. Age alone is not a verdict, but age plus symptoms usually means spending on repairs is buying months, not years.

Signs that usually just need a repair

1. A few missing or wind-lifted shingles after a storm. If the surrounding shingles are healthy, matching and resealing a small area is routine and worth doing quickly.

2. A leak at a penetration. Most leaks are not the roof; they are the flashing around a chimney, vent pipe, or skylight, or a cracked pipe boot. These are targeted fixes.

3. Isolated damage from a fallen limb. Localized impact damage on an otherwise healthy roof is exactly what repairs are for.

The honest math

A fair rule of thumb: if a repair costs a meaningful fraction of replacement and the roof is already showing age symptoms, replacement usually wins, because you keep paying for repairs while the roof keeps aging. If the roof is young and the damage is localized, repair wins. Anyone who recommends replacement without walking you through photos of your actual roof is selling, not diagnosing.
